# Stock screener, peers and valuation history

`scripts/screener.py` screens Indian stocks and provides the peer and valuation-history helpers used by `stock_analysis.py`. Research only.

## Commands

```text
python scripts/screener.py --universe nifty50 --where "roe>15" --where "pe<25" --sort roe
python scripts/screener.py --universe it --sort pe --ascending
python scripts/screener.py --universe psubank --where "dividend_yield>3" --format json --out reports/psu-yield.json
python scripts/screener.py --universe nifty100 --where "dividend_yield>2.5" --where "debt_to_equity<0.5" --sort dividend_yield
python scripts/screener.py --universe nifty50 niftynext50 --universe it --sort roe
python scripts/screener.py --symbols RELIANCE TCS INFY HDFCBANK --sort return_1y
python scripts/screener.py --universe nifty100 --preset dividend --limit 10
python scripts/screener.py --universe it --columns pe roe payout_ratio dividend_years
python scripts/stock_analysis.py --compare TCS INFY HCLTECH --summary
```

`--where` filters combine with AND; repeat it for each condition. Operators are `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `=`. Percent fields (`roe`, margins, growth, yields, `return_1y`, `from_high`) accept `15`, `15%` or `0.15`. `--sort FIELD` sorts descending unless `--ascending`; ties are broken by symbol, so the same data always gives the same order. `--limit` caps the rows shown (default 25). Every field you filter or sort on is added to the table when it is not already a default column. `--columns FIELD ...` (space- or comma-separated) chooses the table columns without a dummy filter.

**`--preset dividend`** screens for steady payers with low debt: dividend yield excluding specials of 1.5% or more, dividends in at least five financial years in a row, and debt/equity of 0.5 or less, sorted by the ex-special yield. It shows price, market cap, both yields, payout, free-cash-flow cover, years paid in a row, 5-year dividend-per-share growth, debt/equity, debt in ₹ crore, ROE and 1-year return. Extra `--where` filters are added to the preset's. With `--limit 10` or fewer it keeps at most two stocks per sector (the Nifty 100 screen of 18 Nov 2026 matched 14, six of them IT) and names those it left out; `--max-per-sector K` sets the cap for any screen (0 = none). Without a cap, a sector holding at least three and more than a third of the rows shown gets a "⚠ Concentrated" line.

**Sector and debt columns.** Every table shows each stock's sector (the "Industry" field of NSE's index list; for `--symbols`, looked up in the Nifty 500 list, one request). `debt_cr` is total debt in ₹ crore on the same balance sheet as debt/equity (Yahoo's figure usually includes lease liabilities): ONGC's 0.46 is ₹1,83,002 cr of debt, ITC's 0.01 is ₹491 cr, so "low debt" can be judged both ways.

**Financials and debt filters.** Debt/equity means nothing for a bank or NBFC (deposits and borrowings are their raw material). When a screen filters on `debt_to_equity`, banks and financials (Yahoo statements with no current assets or EBITDA, or NSE industry "Financial Services") are left out and listed: "Banks/financials excluded: debt/equity not meaningful (...)". They are not silently failed.

## Universes

Constituents come from NSE's published index lists (`nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/indices/ind_<index>list.csv`): `nifty50`, `niftynext50`, `nifty100`, `nifty200`, `nifty500`, `midcap150`, `smallcap250`, and sectors `bank`, `psubank`, `it`, `pharma`, `healthcare`, `auto`, `fmcg`, `metal`, `realty`, `energy`, `oilgas`, `media`, `consumerdurables`. Lists are fetched on every run, so they follow index changes. `nifty100` and `nifty200` use NSE's own `ind_nifty100list.csv` and `ind_nifty200list.csv`.

`--universe` takes several names (`--universe nifty50 niftynext50`, or repeat the flag). The lists are merged in order and each stock is screened once. NSE placeholder entries are dropped: symbols starting `DUMMY` with ISINs starting `DUM`, which NSE uses to hold an index slot during a corporate action (seen: `DUMMYHEG` in the Nifty 500 in November 2026). REIT and InvIT units (series `RR`/`IV`) stay in screens but are never used as peers.

## Metrics

For each stock: price and date, 1-year return, distance from the 52-week high, RSI(14) and dividend yield from Yahoo's daily chart; P/E, P/B, ROE, net and operating margin, debt/equity, revenue and EPS growth, free-cash-flow yield, cash dividends paid / market cap, and market cap from Yahoo's fundamentals timeseries (the same calculation as `stock_analysis.py`). Companies that Yahoo shows in another currency (Infosys in US dollars) are converted to rupees period by period: revenue, profit, EPS and cash flows at that period's average USD/INR from Yahoo's daily `USDINR=X` history (annual and trailing figures over 365 days, quarters over 91), balance-sheet items at the rate on the balance-sheet date. Growth is therefore rupee growth: converting every year at today's rate showed Infosys FY2026 revenue +4.6% (its dollar growth) against about +9.6% in rupees. Because dollar EPS arrives rounded to cents, rupee EPS is rebuilt from rupee profit ÷ diluted average shares. Reports label such figures "converted from USD at period rates". Annual revenue and EPS growth of such companies come from their rupee results filed on NSE when both years load (integrated-filing XBRL, consolidated; the list is paged 20 rows at a time, so the year before last is read from page 2): Infosys FY2026 revenue ₹1,78,650 cr against ₹1,62,990 cr, +9.6% (the period-rate conversion gave +9.3%), and diluted EPS ₹71.46 against ₹64.34, +11.1%. The report then says "rupee results filed on NSE"; screens fetch those filings only when a growth column is shown. If the rate history cannot be fetched, today's rate is used and the report says growth is in USD terms (and the P/E band is skipped).

**Dividend record** (`payout_ratio`, `fcf_cover`, `dividend_years`, `dps_cagr_5y`): payout is the last financial year's cash dividends paid ÷ that year's net profit; FCF cover is that year's free cash flow ÷ dividends paid (below 1× means dividends exceeded free cash flow). Years paid in a row and 5-year dividend-per-share growth come from ten years of Yahoo dividend events (one small monthly-chart request per stock, made only when these fields are filtered, sorted or shown), grouped by the financial year of the ex-date; because Yahoo keeps ten years, 10 means at least 10. A single empty year between paid years is checked against NSE's corporate actions (one request, only then): if NSE shows a dividend that year the streak continues through it; if not, the streak stands but is marked ‡ under the table, "streak may be understated (data gap FY2019-20 ...)" (Hindustan Zinc: Yahoo and NSE show no ex-date between April 2019 and March 2020, with payments in the years either side). The 5-year growth compares two-year averages (FY n−1 and n against FY n−6 and n−5), because a final dividend can fall in either financial year depending on its ex-date. `recurring_yield` is the trailing yield without the special part of payments flagged as possible specials. Payout uses cash paid in the year, so it lags declarations by up to a year; Screener.in's payout (declared dividend ÷ EPS) can differ by a few points.

**RSI(14)** is Wilder's (as Kite and TradingView show), smoothed over the whole history, not a plain 14-day sum (that read 98.1 for Reliance on 18 Nov 2026 against Wilder's 74.3). During market hours today's daily bar is still forming, so RSI and the volume ratio use completed sessions only and say which day.

**1-year return** has one definition everywhere: the latest close against the close 250 sessions earlier. The screener uses two years of daily bars and stock analysis uses five, through the same function, so both show the same figure for the same stock. A stock with fewer than 251 sessions has no 1-year return.

**Dividend yield** (`dividend_yield`) is the dividends per share with an ex-date in the last 12 months ÷ today's price, from Yahoo's chart dividend events. Their amounts are already on today's share count after splits and bonuses (checked on KOTAKBANK's 2026 split and TRENT's 2026 bonus). It is 0 for a stock with a full year of history and no dividend, and missing for a newer listing.

A payment is flagged as a possible special when, among at least four payments in two years, it is more than three times the median of the others. Two exceptions avoid false flags:

- A payment of similar size (at least two-thirds) came about a year earlier or later, and the company pays fewer than about four times a year. That is a regular large final or interim.
- The flagged payments would be over 80% of two years' dividends.

A flagged payment is often a regular interim and a special declared together, so only the part above a regular payment counts as special. The regular amount is the unflagged payment in the same season a year earlier, or else the median of the other payments. On the Nifty 200 in November 2026 this flagged TCS and ABB:

- TCS, January 2026: ₹57 = ₹11 regular interim + ₹46 special. The median of the other payments is ₹12, so ₹45 is treated as special. Trailing yield 4.7%; without the special part about 2.8% (Screener.in 2.69%). Stripping the whole ₹57 gave 2.3%.
- ABB, August 2026: ₹90 against a ₹9.77 interim the August before, so ₹80.23 is treated as special.

**Recent ex-dates.** When a screen shows a yield column, a stock whose latest dividend went ex in the last 30 days is marked † and listed under the table with the amount and ex-date (for example ONGC ₹8.00 on 12 Nov 2026), because a buyer today does not receive it even though it counts in the 12-month yield.

**Definitions under the table** are printed only for the columns the screen shows.

`cash_dividend_yield` keeps the older measure: last financial year's cash dividends paid ÷ today's market cap. It includes specials and can lag a year. Stock analysis shows both, labelled.

**Missing data versus failed to load.** A stock has "missing data" when the feed answered but has no value for a filtered field. It "failed to load" when a request needed for a filter did not succeed (network error, rate limit, cut-off or malformed reply). Requests to the live feed are retried twice with a short backoff (0.5 s, then 1.5 s), and at most four stocks are fetched at once (six for a live screen of more than 100 stocks). A screen run twice therefore gives the same result unless the data itself changed. The header counts both groups. The notes list stocks whose prices failed separately from stocks whose financial statements failed. A stock that passes price-only filters but whose statements failed is shown and listed as "shown without financial statements".

**Renamed symbols and demergers.** Symbols in `--symbols` follow `assets/symbol_aliases.json` the same way as stock analysis (see below). For a company that demerged a business (from `assets/demergers.json`), prices before the demerger are made comparable as described below, and the table notes it when that falls inside the last year. A resulting company in its first partial financial year (TMCV in FY2026) shows no annual growth, with the reason under the table.

## Peers in stock analysis

`stock_analysis.py` compares a stock with its peers. Conglomerates and groups listed in `assets/business_peers.json` (Reliance, ITC, Grasim, Bajaj Finserv; checked 18 Nov 2026) get curated business peers by default, labelled "Business peers": Reliance against IOC and BPCL (refining and fuel retail), BHARTIARTL (telecom), DMART and TRENT (retail), instead of the Nifty Oil & Gas list whose P/Es ran from 3.7 to 102. `--peers` still overrides. The same file lists listed holdings (Reliance → Jio Platforms; Grasim → UltraTech and Aditya Birla Capital; M&M, L&T, HDFC Bank, SBI, ICICI Bank, ITC, Bajaj Finserv): each holding's latest NSE shareholding pattern gives the parent's stake (promoter entities matching the parent's name; the pattern filed on listing for a new listing) and its market cap comes from Yahoo, in the background alongside peers and news. The report prints "Sum of parts (listed holdings at market value, before any holding-company discount)" with the stake × market cap, its share of the parent's market cap and what is left for the rest of the business, with that part's earnings multiple (the parent's trailing earnings less the holdings' share of theirs; an estimate). Reliance, 18 Nov 2026: 64.48% of JPL × ₹12.79 lakh cr = ₹8.24 lakh cr, 45% of Reliance's ₹18.44 lakh cr, leaving ₹10.20 lakh cr at about 16.5× earnings. Stakes and values are never stored in the file.

For a few well-known cases where NSE's sectoral index mixes different businesses, the peers are a short curated list of companies in the same NSE industry classification (`SUB_INDUSTRIES` in `screener.py`, checked 18 Nov 2026 against Screener.in's industry pages, which use that classification):

| Group | Companies | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial vehicles | TMCV, ASHOKLEY, SMLMAH, plus EICHERMOT for its Volvo Eicher trucks and buses | `screener.in/market/IN07/IN0702/IN070202/IN070202002/` |
| Passenger cars and utility vehicles | MARUTI, M&M, HYUNDAI, TMPV, FORCEMOT | `screener.in/market/IN02/IN0201/IN020101/IN020101001/` |
| 2/3 wheelers | BAJAJ-AUTO, EICHERMOT, TVSMOTOR, HEROMOTOCO, ATHERENERG, OLAELEC | `screener.in/market/IN02/IN0201/IN020101/IN020101002/` |

Nifty Auto put TMCV's trucks next to two-wheeler and car makers (peer median P/E 47.8 in November 2026); its own industry gives ASHOKLEY, SMLMAH and EICHERMOT. These are labelled "Industry peers" and the summary names them. Otherwise the peer group is the smallest NSE sectoral index containing the stock (for example Nifty Bank for a bank). If no sectoral index contains it, the group is the stock's Nifty 500 industry, with companies of comparable size first:

- Size tiers come from NSE's own lists: large = Nifty 100, mid = Midcap 150, otherwise small.
- The adjacent tier is added only when the stock's own tier has fewer than five other companies.
- If the tier lists cannot be fetched, the whole industry is used.

The group is capped at 40. Placeholder entries and REIT/InvIT units are never peers. Every peer that loads is used for the group medians, and the largest six by market cap are listed. Index membership is a rough proxy for business similarity, so the report says so. `--peers SYMBOL ...` replaces the group with companies you choose. Use `--no-peers` to skip.

## Renamed symbols and demergers

`assets/symbol_aliases.json` maps NSE symbols that no longer trade to their successors. Each entry has a one-line reason, the date and a source (for example `ZOMATO` → `ETERNAL`, `MCDOWELL-N` → `UNITDSPR`, `TATAMOTORS` → `TMPV` and `TMCV`). The map is used only when the old symbol fails to load or its history stopped more than 14 days ago, so a symbol NSE later reuses is not redirected.

- **One successor:** the report analyses it and says so at the top.
- **Several successors:** the run stops and lists each with what it is, so you can choose one or analyse each. For example, `TATAMOTORS` became `TMPV` (passenger vehicles and JLR) and `TMCV` (commercial vehicles, now named Tata Motors Ltd). With `--format json` the list is printed as `"status": "SYMBOL_CHANGED"` with `successors`.

Yahoo adjusts some demerged parents' past prices and not others. Reliance (2023), ITC (2025) and Siemens (2025) show no jump on the record date; `TMPV.NS` still includes the pre-demerger Tata Motors prices (₹660.75 on 13 Oct 2025, then ₹395.45 on 14 Oct), as do ABFRL (−67% on 22 May 2025) and VEDL (−65% on 30 Apr 2026). For a parent listed in `assets/demergers.json` (under its current or former symbol) the whole history is kept:

- **Already adjusted** (no jump on the ex-date): nothing is changed; the report says so in a note. A note about a demerger more than a year old sits under Notes in the full report and is left out of `--summary` (Reliance's 2023 Jio Financial demerger changes nothing there).
- In `--summary` a scaling note is one plain sentence ("Yahoo does not adjust prices before the 2025-10-14 demerger of Tata Motors Limited (TMCV); they are scaled down so past and present compare like with like."), kept while the demerger is inside the two years behind the outlook ranges; the factor and the cost-split detail stay in the full report.
- **Unadjusted, small demerger** (under 10% of cost, e.g. HUL → Kwality Wall's 1.91%): prices and dividends before the ex-date are multiplied by the parent's published cost split (0.9809).
- **Unadjusted, large demerger:** they are multiplied by that day's own price ratio (TMPV 0.598), which is how the market valued the parts. The published cost split is a tax apportionment of book value and can be far from it (ABFRL kept 75.68% of cost but about 33% of the price), so it is not used as a price factor here.
- The ex-date is `ex_date` when given (VEDL: 30 Apr 2026, because the 1 May record date was a holiday), else the record date.
- For a large demerger the P/E band uses only annual EPS for years ending after the record date; for a small one all years are used and the basis says so.

**Statements around a demerger.** With `appointed_date`/`effective_date` and `resulting_first_full_fy_end` in the list:

- A parent whose compared financial years straddle the appointed date gets no annual growth ("FY2026 vs FY2025 straddles the demerger ..."); the latest quarter against a year earlier is used instead.
- A resulting company before its first full financial year (TMCV until FY2027) gets no annual growth, annual ROE or P/E band (TMCV's FY2026 revenue +44% compared part of a year of trucks with the pre-demerger company); ROE and margins use the last four quarters when all of them follow the appointed date.
- A year-on-year quarter comparison is withheld when the year-ago quarter predates the appointed date.

**Single-day falls.** Any single-session fall of more than 35% with no split recorded is reported as a possible unadjusted corporate action. Stock analysis also checks falls of more than 4% in the last two years against NSE's corporate actions for the symbol (one request, made only when such a fall exists): a fall on an ex-date of a bonus, split, rights issue or similar that Yahoo did not record is flagged; a fall mostly explained by a large dividend is named as such; an ordinary dividend on a day the market fell is not flagged.

## Comparing stocks

`stock_analysis.py --compare SYM SYM ... [--summary] [--format json]` (2 to 8 symbols) runs the single-stock analysis for each (no news or peer groups; the Nifty history and USD/INR rates are fetched once for all) and prints one table: price, ROE, operating margin (EBITDA ÷ revenue), net margin, debt/equity, P/E (TTM or FY), P/E against the stock's own history ("cheaper than 88% of days since May 2023"), rupee revenue and EPS growth, latest quarter profit against the same quarter a year earlier, 1-year return and dividend yield excluding specials. The footnote states the window the P/E history really covers (since Nov 2021 for TCS, Infosys and HCLTech in November 2026, with older annual EPS from NSE's annual results filings; since May 2023 when that back-fill fails, as far as Yahoo's four annual EPS figures go). EBITDA ÷ revenue is the closest free match to Screener.in's OPM; it includes other income, so it can read a point or two higher (November 2026: TCS 27.1% against 27%, HCLTech 21.0% against 21%, Infosys 25.3% against 24%). Above the table, one trade-off line per dimension ranks the stocks best first: quality (ROE, net margin), value (P/E today, P/E against its own history with the 5-year median), growth (rupee revenue and EPS growth, latest quarter; demerger-affected companies are left out and named), momentum (1-year return), income (dividend yield excluding specials) and balance sheet (debt/equity with its balance-sheet date; banks and financials left out; when every debt/equity is below 0.15 the line says "all effectively debt-free" and lists them instead of ranking 0.05 against 0.10). There is deliberately no tally of "wins": measures inside a dimension move together (a low P/E today is usually also low against history), so counting them double-counts and nudges towards one stock. A one-offs line follows when a quarter in trailing earnings had large exceptional items, negative other income or other income far above its usual level, and another when the latest year's annual EPS includes one-offs (exceptional items of 2% of the year's profit or more after tax, or such an other-income spike), with EPS growth estimated without them (TCS FY2026 +1.4% reported, about +8.4% without the September and December 2025 exceptional charges; the table's EPS cell reads "+1.4% (≈+8.4% ex one-offs)"). The table also shows the latest quarter's revenue against a year earlier and against the quarter before (NSE filings). Growth is in rupees: constant-currency growth and guidance come from the companies' results releases. The lines end with "No single stock wins on every measure" (or, when one stock ranks first everywhere, a reminder to check the cautions). Cautions (one-offs, specials, demerger notes, currency) follow the table. There is no single pick.

A symbol that became several companies (`TATAMOTORS`) shows the successors side by side in the same way instead of stopping; `--format json` still returns `"status": "SYMBOL_CHANGED"`.

## Latest quarter, profit swings and one-offs

The latest quarter is compared with the same quarter a year earlier, not only the previous quarter. Yahoo's quarterly series skip quarters (in November 2026 it had no September 2025 quarter for Reliance, TCS or HCLTech), so stock analysis reads both quarters from NSE's integrated filings (results XBRL; consolidated when filed, profit attributable to shareholders, always in rupees) and says so. A change across a sign is described ("swung from ₹775 cr profit to ₹5,531 cr loss"), never given as a percentage. A year-ago quarter more than three times a typical quarter is flagged as a probable one-off. When profit swung sign and the year-ago quarter's own filing shows one-offs pushing the same way (exceptional items of at least a quarter of the profit, negative other income, or tax far from a normal rate, such as tax charged on a pre-tax loss), the report says "the year-ago quarter was distorted by one-offs; the swing overstates the turnaround" (TMCV, September 2025 quarter: ₹306 cr of tax on a ₹549 cr pre-tax loss, a ₹867 cr net loss; Screener.in shows other income of −₹1,865 cr for it).

When a year's net profit does not match diluted EPS × diluted shares by more than 35% (TMPV FY2026: ₹82,390 cr, about ₹224 a share, against continuing EPS of −₹0.61), the report says the profit includes discontinued operations or a one-off. When that or a large annual EPS swing fires, ROE and net margin use the last four quarters and are labelled "trailing 12 months"; the annual figure stays in the JSON as `roe_annual`.

**Book value** for P/B is the newest balance sheet (half-yearly in India; shareholders' equity excluding minority interest, as Screener.in uses), with its date in the table. The March figure alone overstated P/B by about 10% for TCS (8.07 against Screener.in's 7.3) and 20% for TMCV (12.8 against 10.7) in November 2026.

**Debt/equity** uses the newest balance sheet that reports both debt and equity: Yahoo's quarterly series carries the 30 September one, which the annual series does not. The summary always prints the date ("debt/equity 0.84 at 30 Sep 2026" for TMPV, against 0.73 at 31 Mar 2026). When NSE's latest results filing has a newer balance sheet than Yahoo, borrowings ÷ equity attributable to shareholders from that filing are used instead (lease liabilities not included) and the basis says so. Screens use the same figure.

**Exceptional items and one-offs.** When the latest quarter's exceptional items are at least a fifth of its profit or loss, the summary's first caution says so with the pre-tax profit before them: "The latest quarter's loss attributable to shareholders (₹5,531 cr, quarter to 2026-09-30) includes ₹6,738 cr of exceptional charges; profit before exceptional items and tax was ₹403 cr (including the ₹29 cr share of joint ventures and associates, which the filing adds after tax; ₹374 cr before it)" (TMPV). When the XBRL adds the share of joint ventures and associates after tax (profit for the period = continuing operations + that share), the share is included in the pre-exceptional profit, which then matches the "PBT (bei)" in Tata Motors' results release, and the figure before it is given too. The three quarters before the latest are also read from NSE's filings; a quarter whose exceptional items or negative other income are at least 10% of its profit is listed as a one-off inside trailing earnings, with the trailing P/E without them (before any tax effect). TCS's December 2025 quarter: ₹3,391 cr of exceptional charges; other income net of them is −₹2,273 cr, the figure Screener.in shows as other income. Screener.in's trailing P/E for TCS (15.8 on 18 Nov 2026) is close to the report's P/E without one-offs (15.7), not its headline 16.8.

**One-off gains in other income.** Stock analysis reads up to eight quarters from NSE's integrated filings (all on the list's first page, read in parallel) and flags a quarter whose other income is at least 1.5× the median of the other quarters, with the excess at least 10% of that quarter's pre-tax profit, unless another quarter is within 70% of it (lumpy or stepped-up income: Adani Ports) or the same quarter a year apart is within 60% (seasonal dividends). Banks are not checked. Reliance's June 2025 quarter: ₹15,119 cr against a usual ₹4,910 cr (the ₹8,924 cr Asian Paints stake-sale gain sat in other income, not exceptional items). Across the Nifty 100 on 18 Nov 2026 the rule flagged six quarters: Reliance (Jun 2025), Titan (Sep 2026, a ₹671 cr fair-value gain per its release), DLF, Adani Power, GAIL and Jindal Steel; the wording says such a jump is often a one-off and to check the results release. A spike inside the trailing twelve months joins the trailing one-offs; one in the latest or year-ago quarter gets the year-on-year change without it.

**One-offs inside annual EPS.** When the quarters of the latest financial year carry exceptional items or an other-income spike worth at least 2% of the year's profit after tax (taxed at the company's usual effective rate, the median over the quarters read), the report gives that year's EPS, EPS growth and the P/E-history position without them, as estimates; the year before loses its own exceptional items (full-year figures from its March filing). Reliance FY2026: EPS ₹59.69 reported, about ₹53.97 without the June 2025 gain; growth +16.0% reported, about +4.9% without it; P/E on that EPS 25.3, cheaper than on 68% of days since Nov 2021 against 22.8 and 88% on the reported EPS. TCS FY2026: +1.4% reported, about +8.4% without the September and December 2025 exceptional charges (restructuring in September; in December ₹2,128 cr for the new labour codes, ₹1,010 cr of legal provision and ₹253 cr of restructuring). The note is left out when the figures barely move (growth within a point, the P/E-history position within five percentile points and, with no growth to compare, EPS within 5%): Grasim's FY2026 growth was +31.8% either way because FY2025 had similar charges. A sum of parts worth more than the parent (Grasim: UltraTech and Aditya Birla Capital stakes at 128% of its market cap) is worded as the market valuing the rest at nothing, with the implied discount on the stakes.

**P/E beside the P/E without one-offs.** When the trailing year's one-offs move the P/E by more than 10%, the summary's valuation line gives both: "P/E 32.4 (trailing 12 months), about 46.1 without one-offs (before tax; see ⚠)" (Hindustan Unilever, 19 Nov 2026, with ₹4,485 cr of profit from the demerged ice-cream business; Screener.in showed 43.6). Adani Enterprises read 58.1 with its December 2025 stake-sale gain and about 110 without (Screener.in 114); Sun Pharma 41.6 and about 35.8 (37.0).

**Share count after a QIP.** Market cap uses the latest NSE shareholding pattern's share count plus equity allotted since in NSE's corporate announcements (QIP, rights issue, ESOPs, conversions; never NCDs, debentures, bonds or commercial paper) when that differs from Yahoo's by more than 1%. Bajaj Finance, 19 Nov 2026: 622.60 cr shares at 30 Sep 2026 plus the 11.41 cr QIP shares of 17 Nov 2026 gave ₹6.93 lakh cr against Yahoo's ₹6.80 lakh cr. A preferential issue of "securities" (5.74 cr on 10 Nov 2026) may be warrants, so it is named and not counted. P/B stays on the pattern's count until a balance sheet shows the new capital.

**Gaps in Yahoo's years and a newer year on NSE.** Annual growth and average equity use only consecutive financial years (300-430 days apart). Yahoo's Nestlé India years were Dec 2021, Dec 2022 and Mar 2025 (the 15-month FY2024 missing, FY2026 not yet added), which had given "revenue +19.6%" for FY2025 against Dec 2022 and ROE 99%. When NSE's March-quarter filings reach a newer year than Yahoo, ROE, net margin and annual growth use them, the year before's EPS put on today's share basis (Nestlé FY2026: revenue ₹23,155 cr, +14.6%; ROE 76.3%, Screener.in 74.2%), and the newer EPS joins the P/E history.

**Back-fill cache.** Live runs keep each parsed NSE annual results filing in the per-user cache folder ($INDIA_DAY_TRADER_CACHE, else %LOCALAPPDATA%/india-day-trader/cache or ~/.cache/india-day-trader; "off" disables it) and the annual-results list for up to 120 days, so a slow exchange on a later run does not cut the P/E history short. A filing never changes once published.

**Profit attributable to shareholders.** The latest-quarter line is labelled "profit attributable to shareholders". Press reports usually quote total profit including minority interests, so that is added when it differs by half a point or more (Reliance, September 2026 quarter: +40.6% for shareholders, +33.8% in total).

**Amounts** are in crore with Indian digit grouping (₹1,78,650 cr); market cap of one lakh crore or more is in lakh crore (₹18.44 lakh cr). Screen tables group market cap the Indian way too (2,39,114).

## News headlines

Stock analysis lists the last week's headlines from Google News, Yahoo and NSE. Stock-tip lists, price targets, many-stock round-ups, option-activity items and quote pages ("Reliance Industries Limited (RELIANCE.NS) stock price, news, quote and history") are left out ("Six Stocks To Buy Today ..." was Reliance's latest headline on 18 Nov 2026), as are headlines that do not name the company, or name only its sister company from the same demerger (a TMPV price target under TMCV; `news_terms` in `assets/symbol_aliases.json` tell the two apart). The report says how many were left out. The summary's "Latest headline" and the full report's "Latest headline about the company" are the same item: the newest story the company leads or an NSE filing, before a market round-up that only mentions it, and before routine items (a broker conference "update", an analyst or investor meet schedule, a record-date or newspaper-advertisement filing), which stay in the list but are not picked (Reliance's summary on 18 Nov 2026 had led with "... JM Financial Conference Update · Kalkine India"); ties are broken by headline so reruns pick the same one. Headlines are not verified facts.

## P/E history band

`stock_analysis.py` shows the stock's P/E over the last five years: price on each session divided by the latest annual diluted EPS that had been published by then (each EPS is used from 60 days after its financial year end). It reports the years and sessions actually covered, the low, the 25th–75th percentile range, the median, the high and where today's P/E sits, plus the 5-year median and the median over the longest history available (10-year when ten years load, otherwise "median since Jun 2019").

Yahoo keeps only about four annual EPS figures (since May 2023 in November 2026), so older years come from NSE's annual results filings: the list at `https://www.nseindia.in/api/corporates-financial-results?index=equities&symbol=SYMBOL&period=Annual` and each year's XBRL (`INDAS_*.xml`, FY2019 onwards; earlier years are PDFs and are not read). Consolidated filings are used unless fewer than two consolidated years exist (Reliance filed FY2022 standalone only; its standalone EPS is a different business). The full-year figures are read from the cumulative column by name because those files' context dates are wrong or missing (Infosys FY2024's says January–March). Each filing's EPS is divided by every split or bonus Yahoo records after the filing date (Reliance's 1:1 bonus of October 2024 halves FY2019–FY2024), and years Yahoo also has must agree within 15%, which catches an unrecorded split or a different basis; otherwise nothing is added and the report says why. A year with no figure leaves a gap (the sessions it would cover are left out) instead of stretching the year before. Prices before the five-year history come from Yahoo's ten-year chart, scaled at the join so a demerger adjustment carries back. For a large demerger only years after the record date count. The back-fill runs alongside the core data with a 15-second limit (parallel requests; parsed filings are kept for the rest of the run, since a published filing never changes); if it fails or times out, the Yahoo-only band is shown with its start date ("since May 2023") and the full report names the reason.

Today's value in the band is on the same basis, latest annual EPS. The headline P/E usually uses trailing 12-month EPS, so the summary and the report show the two separately and never compare the trailing figure with the band. The position is said plainly: "P/E 22.5 (trailing 12 months); on latest annual EPS 22.8, cheaper than on 88% of trading days since Nov 2021 (usual range 24.5–29.4; 5-year median 27.3, median since May 2019 27.1)".

Sessions after a loss year are left out, and an older profitable year is not reused. There is no band when the latest annual EPS is a loss, or for a resulting company before its first full financial year. Financials converted at period exchange rates keep their band; it is skipped only when today's rate had to be used.

**Peer medians.** The peer median P/E is not printed when the peers' P/Es span more than 5× (Reliance's Nifty Oil & Gas peers ran from 3.8 to 102 in November 2026); the summary lists the lowest and highest instead and the table says "too spread". A P/E below its history can reflect lower expected growth, not only cheapness.

The outlook ranges also state the years and sessions they use: up to two years, less after a demerger or for a recent listing. Bull-case reference levels are listed nearest first going up, bear-case levels nearest first going down, and two levels within 0.5% of each other are merged. The wording follows the trend: in an uptrend a close above the 20-day high extends the move and one below the 20-day low weakens it; in a downtrend a close above the high would break the downtrend and one below the low extends the decline; in a mixed trend they break the range up or down. When price is within half an ATR(14) of either level the summary says "price is testing ₹X now" (TMPV at ₹252.50 against a ₹252.30 low, November 2026). The summary prints up to six cautions in full; beyond that, the rest are named by their opening clause on one "Also:" line, never just counted.

## Data checks

Each stock's trailing EPS is cross-checked against its last four consecutive quarters:

- If Yahoo's trailing figure differs from their sum by more than 25%, the sum is used and flagged.
- **Splits and bonuses inside the trailing year.** Quarters that ended before a split or bonus (Yahoo split events, plus any bonus in `assets/bonuses.json` that Yahoo did not record) are divided by the share ratio only when their own figures show they were not restated (EPS × today's shares ≈ ratio × that quarter's net profit). Independently, trailing EPS is compared with trailing net profit ÷ today's share count (market cap ÷ price). When they differ by more than 10% and a split or bonus falls inside the trailing year, or the split-adjusted quarters agree with the net-profit figure, the net-profit figure is used and flagged: TRENT on 18 Nov 2026 had Yahoo trailing EPS ₹27.99 after its 1:2 bonus (record date 4 Jun 2026) against ₹1,896 cr ÷ 53.32 cr shares = ₹35.55, so P/E 89 (Screener.in about 89–90) instead of 114. Otherwise the reported EPS is kept and the gap is flagged, since it usually means discontinued operations, a one-off below the EPS line or new shares issued in the year (Nifty 100, November 2026: ADANIENT after its rights issue, SHRIRAMFIN at about 10%). The same check runs in single-stock reports, peer rows, comparisons and screens; the P/E basis then reads "trailing 12 months, net profit ÷ current shares" (TTM in the comparison table).
- If one quarter makes trailing EPS differ from four typical quarters by more than half, the P/E is flagged with the typical-quarter P/E.
- A smaller distortion is mentioned (never corrected) when all of these hold: one quarter is below 40% of the median of the other three, those three agree within 1.6×, and the same quarter a year earlier is in the data and was normal. The last condition keeps seasonal businesses with a weak quarter every year, such as hydro power, from being flagged. On the Nifty 200 in November 2026 this check flagged two stocks (BPCL's loss quarter and Biocon) and none of TCS, Infosys or Reliance.
- An annual EPS change above +200% or below −60% is flagged as a possible one-off.
- **Revenue that does not match the filings.** Yahoo's revenue for finance companies is often net of interest cost or leaves out part of income (Jio Financial FY2026: +136% and a 49% net margin on Yahoo; Bajaj Finance's September 2026 quarter ₹14,965 cr against ₹24,546 cr filed). When annual revenue more than doubles, or Yahoo's latest-quarter revenue is more than 10% away from the NSE filing's, stock analysis reads full-year revenue and profit from the March-quarter NSE filings and uses them for revenue growth and net margin (Bajaj Finance FY2026: ₹81,982 cr against ₹69,684 cr). If the earlier year is not available as an integrated filing (Jio Financial FY2025), growth is not shown. Screens and peer rows, which do not read filings, withhold revenue growth and margins when revenue more than doubled, with a note.
- Operating profit above EBITDA cannot be right (Yahoo's HCLTech FY2026 operating income gave a 31% margin against EBITDA of 21%); EBIT is used instead.

Flagged stocks get ⚠ in the table and a note under it, as do possible special dividends. When banks or finance companies appear in a screen filtered on ROE or P/B, a note warns that Yahoo's book equity can differ a lot from the net worth in the bank's own results. The header shows the price date; if Yahoo's latest daily close was missing, the last-trade quote is used and counted in the header.

## Limits

Yahoo's financial data can be stale, restated or missing, especially for smaller companies. Ratios are from reported annual figures and the latest trailing EPS. EPS growth uses diluted EPS (profit ÷ weighted diluted shares); Screener.in divides by year-end shares, so after a buyback it shows a little more (Infosys FY2026: +11.1% from its reported diluted EPS, about +12.9% on Screener.in). Rupee conversion for dollar reporters uses daily average market rates, so it can differ from the company's own rupee statements by a few tenths of a percent. The alias and demerger lists are curated and dated; a change after their `checked` date is not known until an entry is added. Nothing here is a recommendation or authorizes an order.

## Time limits and gaps

Every live request has a per-operation timeout (15 s for prices, statements and NSE filings; 8 s for news and peers), and transient failures are retried twice with a short backoff, never stacked on the public fetcher's own retries. News and the peer group load in the background while the core data loads and share one 30-second budget: when it runs out, no new request starts and the summary says "Data gaps: peers (timed out)" or "news (timed out)". When every news feed fails the summary says "Data gaps: news (source unavailable)" instead of leaving the headline line out silently. Nothing extra is printed to standard output.

## Split symbols and new listings

`stock_analysis.py TATAMOTORS --summary` explains the demerger once, then prints the normal summary of each successor (TMPV, TMCV: valuation, peers, trend, levels, headline, cautions) and a compact side-by-side table; the full report mode keeps the comparison table. A stock whose Yahoo first-trade date is within 150 days (Jio Platforms, JPL, listed October 2026) is analysed from five sessions: moving averages, RSI, ranges and 1-year figures stay n/a, the price line gives the move since the first close, and the summary says so. The Valuation line ends with the market cap, which the sum-of-parts check in the stock skill uses with the parent's stake from `shareholding.py`.

## Screen notes

A demerger note under a screen (ITC: "annual growth not shown") appears only when the table shows a figure it withholds (revenue or EPS growth; ROE, margins or P/E too for a company in its first partial year after a demerger). The closing "Ratios come from company financials ..." line appears once.

## Debt columns and threshold precision (November 2026)

- `debt_cr` is labelled **debt incl. leases (₹ cr)**: Yahoo's total debt on the same balance sheet as debt/equity, which includes lease
  liabilities (TCS's ₹11,808 cr at 30 Sep 2026 is all leases). When an NSE results filing supplies a newer balance sheet, the figure is its
  borrowings, which leave leases out.
- `borrowings_cr`, **borrowings ex leases (₹ cr)**, is that debt minus Yahoo's capital-lease obligations for the same date (n/a when Yahoo does
  not give them). It is shown next to `debt_cr` whenever `debt_cr` is shown, and can be filtered on (`--where "borrowings_cr<500"`).
- A value that would print the same as a filter's threshold is printed with up to two more decimals (TCS debt/equity 0.0996, not "0.10", under
  `debt_to_equity<0.1`), so a match never looks like it fails its own filter.

## Peers inside a broad NSE industry

NSE's Nifty 500 "Industry" field is a sector. For "Financial Services" the company name decides the kind of business (banks; insurers; state
infrastructure lenders such as PFC, REC and IRFC; capital-market companies such as AMCs and exchanges; lending companies, NBFCs and housing
finance), and the industry peers are narrowed to the same kind when at least three others are found (`screener.business_kind`). The curated
lists in `assets/business_peers.json` (Bajaj Finance against other NBFCs; Eternal and Swiggy against each other) come first.

## Sector exclusion, run time and saved snapshots (November 2026)

- `--exclude-sector "Financial Services"` (repeatable, case-insensitive, `&` and `and` alike) leaves out stocks whose NSE sector (the sector column, from the index list's Industry field) matches. They are dropped before anything is fetched, and the report says how many were left out; a sector name no stock in the universe has is reported, so a typo does not pass silently.
- Run time: a Nifty 500 screen took 3 to 4.5 minutes with four stocks at a time (21 Nov 2026). A live screen of more than 100 stocks now fetches six at a time: the same Nifty 500 ROE > 20, P/E < 25 screen without Financial Services (402 stocks) took 90 s with no failures beyond the one stock that also failed before. The report's last lines give the run time.
- Saved snapshots: live runs save each stock's snapshot (prices, ratios and checks) in the per-user cache folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%/india-day-trader/cache/screen_snapshots` on Windows, `$XDG_CACHE_HOME` or `~/.cache` elsewhere; `INDIA_DAY_TRADER_CACHE=off` disables it). A snapshot is reused only outside market hours (09:00 to 16:30 IST on weekdays nothing is read or written), only when it was saved after the last completed session (16:30 IST on the latest weekday) and never after 20 hours; snapshots with failed statements or dividend history are not saved. The same screen rerun with other filters took 4 s. `--no-cache` fetches everything afresh. An exchange holiday only makes the cache miss.
- Demerger parents: a stock whose trailing EPS period began before a large demerger in `assets/demergers.json` (more than 10% of the cost went to the demerged business) gets a "P/E caution" in the earnings checks, with a rough P/E on the first whole quarter after it (×4). Vedanta, November 2026: P/E 4.34 on trailing EPS that includes seven months of the aluminium, power, oil-and-gas and iron-ore businesses demerged on 1 May 2026, about 6.4 on the September 2026 quarter annualised; Screener.in showed 7.05.

## Multi-year growth, consolidated lenders and low ROE in stock analysis (November 2026)

- 3-year CAGR of revenue and of profit attributable to shareholders, from NSE's annual results filings (the two newest years from the March-quarter integrated filings, the older ones from the annual filings already read for the P/E history; both on the same basis, consolidated unless only standalone exists), else from Yahoo's annual statements. Shown in the single-stock summary and ratio table and in `--compare` (a table column and two ranked growth measures). Withheld after a large demerger after the first year; "n/m" across a loss. Asian Paints FY2023 to FY2026: revenue +1.0% a year and profit +1.7% a year, against FY2026 revenue +5.1%.
- Consolidated lenders: when a listed holding in `assets/business_peers.json` is a lender (bank-like statements) the parent owns more than 50% of, and its borrowings (Yahoo, latest balance sheet) are at least a quarter of the parent's consolidated borrowings, a ⚠ names it and its share, and gives debt/equity without it: the lender's borrowings out, and the parent's share of its equity out of equity (an estimate). L&T, 30 Sep 2026: 1.24 consolidated, L&T Finance about 88% of the borrowings, about 0.18 without it.
- Low ROE: when ROE is more than 8 points below the peer median and under 60% of it, one line gives the likely reason from the balance sheet: goodwill and other intangibles of 30% of equity or more (with their growth, which points to acquisitions, and the return on equity without them), or cash and short-term investments of 35% or more; else that neither explains it. Tata Consumer, Sep 2026: goodwill and intangibles ₹18,938 cr, 86% of equity, up from ₹10,875 cr in March 2023.
- The single-stock summary's quality line now gives the latest quarter's revenue against a year earlier beside its profit.
- Holdings lists were audited on 21 Nov 2026 against the promoter entities in the latest NSE shareholding pattern of every Nifty 500 company; added: L&T Finance (L&T), HDB Financial (HDFC Bank), Bajaj Housing Finance (Bajaj Finance), and new parents such as Vedanta (Hindustan Zinc), ONGC (HPCL, MRPL, Petronet LNG), NTPC (NTPC Green), Coal India (Bharat Coking Coal, CMPDI), Bharti Airtel (Bharti Hexacom, Indus Towers), PFC (REC), Bajaj Holdings, Godrej Industries, Tube Investments (CG Power) and others.
